Today the term Third World has bad connotations, but it’s not the original meaning. [The term meant to classify those countries that were not under the USA imperialism or the Soviet Union influence during the Cold War.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_World) Countries there were trying to lead the world to a change, with its main principle as non-interference in other countries' domestic affairs. The USA made it clear it wasn’t going to allow this by overthrowing elected governments and installing dictatorships all over the group, e.g. Brazil, Chile, Argentina, Guatemala. As a result of the USA interference, the movement failed to archive its main goal.  Today the domain over the internet is following the same path, any tentative of escaping the USA-Europe logic is attacked. Maybe it’s time to resurrect the spirit of the Third World and fight it.

According to The Jakarta Method, “the term “Third World”, was born in 1951 in France” but it really solidified the idea during the Konferensi Asia-Afrika. Quoting Wikipedia: “the conference's stated aims were to promote Afro-Asian economic and cultural cooperation and to oppose colonialism or neocolonialism by any nation”.

A  10-point declaration of promotion of world peace and cooperation was one of the results of that Conference:

1. Respect for fundamental human rights and for the purposes and principles of the charter of the United Nations
2. Respect for the sovereignty and territorial integrity of all nations
3. Recognition of the equality of all races and of the equality of all nations large and small
4. Abstention from intervention or interference in the internal affairs of another country
5. Respect for the right of each nation to defend itself, singly or collectively, in conformity with the charter of the United Nations
6. (a) Abstention from the use of arrangements of collective defense to serve any particular interests of the big powers
(b) Abstention by any country from exerting pressures on other countries
7. Refraining from acts or threats of aggression or the use of force against the territorial integrity or political independence of any country
8. Settlement of all international disputes by peaceful means, such as negotiation, conciliation, arbitration or judicial settlement as well as other peaceful means of the parties own choice, in conformity with the charter of the United Nations
9. Promotion of mutual interests and cooperation
10. Respect for justice and international obligation

But what I really would like to highlight from the conference is Sukarno’s speech (the first president of Indonesia):

>All of us, I’m certain, are united by more important things than those which superficially dived us. We are unified, for instance, by a common detestation of colonialism in whatever form it appears. We are united by a common destination of racialism. And we are united by a common determination to preserver and stabilize peace in the world […]

>How is it possible to be disinterested about colonialism? For us, colonialism is not something far and distance. We have known it in all its ruthlessness. We have seen the immense human wastage it causes, the poverty its causes, and the heritage it leaves behind when, eventually and reluctantly, it’s driven out by the inevitable march of history. My people, and the peoples of many nations of Asia and Africa, know these things, for we have experienced them […]

> Yes, some parts of our nations are not yet free. That is why all of us cannot yet feel that journey’s end has been reached […]

> And I beg of you, do not think of colonialism only in the classic form which we of Indonesia, and our brothers in different parts of Asia and Africa, knew. Colonialism has also its modern dress, in the form of economic control, intellectual control, actual physical control by a small alien community within the nation. It is a skilful and determined enemy, and it appears in many guises. It does not give up its loot easily. Wherever, whenever, and however it appears, colonialism is an evil thing, and one which must be eradicated from the earth. […]


## What’s neocolonialism?

According to the [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/neocolonialism), neocolonialism is “political control by a rich country of a poorer country that should be independent and free to govern itself”.  That power is most of the time due to economic dependency:


> We, politely referred to as "underdeveloped", in truth, are colonial, semi-colonial or dependent countries. We are countries whose economies have been distorted by imperialism, which has abnormally developed those branches of industry or agriculture needed to complement its complex economy. "Underdevelopment", or distorted development, brings a dangerous specialisation in raw materials, inherent in which is the threat of hunger for all our peoples. We, the "underdeveloped", are also those with the single crop, the single product, the single market. A single product whose uncertain sale depends on a single market imposing and fixing conditions. That is the great formula for imperialist economic domination. - [Che Guevara](https://www.marxists.org/archive/guevara/1961/04/09.htm)


## Is the Internet being colonised by the Global North?

The internet by default is a very decentralised place. Any computer connected with the internet is able to serve content. We can all stream movies and music stored in our neighbour’s computers. There is zero scarcity of information on the internet. When you consume a text or a video, the bytes are still there for someone else to consume as well. Capitalists don’t like this. 

The biggest asset today is information. But how can you charge someone for this if it’s freely available over the internet? You need somehow to isolate this information. That’s basically what Facebook and Medium are all about. You generate content, your friends react to that content, generating even more information. Facebook processes it and sells a by-product to other corporations. “Capital Is Dead. Is This Something Worse?” goes beyond, it explains that even Walmart is an information company.  They track the products being sold, the needed products, and so on. Without their big datacentres processing all the logistics they would not be where they are now. In her book, McKenzie Wark, invite us to wonder if Capitalism is dead and we are living in a society where the ownership of intellectual property is more important than the ownership of the means of production.

The [Telekommunist](https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Telekommunist_Manifesto) manifesto also goes in that direction, explaining: The Internet represents a powerful platform for new forms of production to emerge; however, the rapid commercialization of the Internet is increasing the centralization of ownership and control of Internet-based communications platforms. This is visible in the consolidation of Internet service providers by massive international telecommunications conglomerates. This rise of Social Media and “Web2.0” have pushed more free, decentralized “peer to peer systems to the clandestine margins of the network while ”cloud computing" further centralizes the infrastructure. If new ways of producing and distributing wealth do not emerge to challenge the capitalist order, ways which are not based on force and enclosure, it is not only the freedom of the Internet that will be lost, but the chance to remake society in it’s image will be lost with it.“

The ownership of good part of the information generated on the internet are in very few hands and centralised in one hemisphere. McKenzie Wark calls those people “Vectoralists”. Although she says the Vectoralist class is not bound by nationality, it’s hard to not relate both knowing that any attempt to fight it from the Global South will become a “national threat” for countries like the USA. As proof, we have the recent attacks on Chinese companies by the government of the USA. The biggest sin they committed was not following the USA leadership. [Because we all know privacy and government interference are not something new around tech](https://www.infoworld.com/article/2608141/snowden--the-nsa-planted-backdoors-in-cisco-products.html).

In order to access services we today find important, we are forced to give the only asset we have. Our own data. More and more companies are recruiting only using LinkedIn, localise your self in a new city requires you to consent to Google/Bing Maps, talk with your friends require you to use whatever social app they are using, buying online gives your data to whatever payment processor the e-commerce is using (and the e-commerce itself). Although we should have the right to govern our data, the control of most data we produce are on big tech hands. Are we being colonised by Big Tech?

I know that in a world where colonialism killed and kills people, worrying about the internet seems silly. But if information is so important for the elite, shouldn’t we worry about its as well? What can a racist police do with all the Ring Cameras people are putting on their doors? What about your location data? How it can be used to track activists? 

Most of our data is stored and controlled by the same countries that installed and supported dictatorships all over the Global South. [They are not ashamed of using it  to collaborate with government institutions](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jaymcgregor/2019/08/06/heres-how-amazons-ring-doorbell-police-partnership-affects-you/#2ddf86c967a6),  the activists, minorities or even opposition politicians should worry about it. But there’s some hope, the recent attacks on Chinese companies show something the USA tried to hide for a long time. They are not invincible and we are approaching a new era. They are afraid of what’s coming. We have two options, help those who slaved us for so long to keep their power or fight back. I don’t believe a single country would be able to win a fight against Silicon Valley, but a group of countries may. Europe’s GDPR is living proof. Silicon Valley was forced to obey the regulation that protects a little bit of our data. Could a group of “Third World” countries force even bigger changes?
