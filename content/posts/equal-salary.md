Since most people in the software development area were working from home in the last months, a lot has being written about going remote for ever. Some big companies already announced they will reduce salary for those living certain areas. What concerns me specially is the difference of wage between someone moving to the Global South. The main argument is “the cost of life is smaller”. What’s not true. Rent and food may be cheaper, but the Global North does include in its own cost of live: electrical devices, books, movies, theatres, going to pubs, dinner in expensive restaurantes and traveling. While for the Global South, eating and sleeping is more than enough. The profit generated from the remote work labour is the same, why capitalists want to reduce so drastically the salary of those returning home?

In the tech field there was always a meritocracy myth. If you collaborate with more, you make more. Something on the lines of “we are all owners of this company”.  Mostly, salary differences across the company were justified around those lines plus something like “different projects/task”. The capitalist class is not able to sustain that lie anymore, when workers move from one country to another without changing positions and their salary decreases, the capitalist class must tell the truth. The wages are only a response of external actors. It’s nothing to do with your capacity, the value you generate or the generosity of the upper class. It’s just external actors. One may call “the market”, but the truth is not only an invisible god ruling through offer and demand, those external actors can be as well: unions, labour rights (conquered with blood), cost of life.

Yes, cost of life is one of the factors, and the reason is simple: the capitalist (still) needs living labour. Labour needs to eat and reproduce. But there’s one more thing, in the software development area there are a lack of workers. The capitalist class then needs two things: increase that pool of workers and keep its own workers from joining the competition. To increase the pool of workers, companies keep doing propaganda about how “developers are cool”, meritocracy and high salaries. Besides that there’s the myth of the creative people. As the creative people were fighting to change the world for better and getting the rewards for that. “Work hard, make history”, “edit the company”, “act like an owner”. Big tech is full of slogans showing that. 

The second reason: avoid workers from joining the competition is more complex. IT companies have huge budgets to keep its developers happy: beers, food, video-games. How much does cost those big fancy offices? Of course, is not only about treating the developers as children. Salary is one of the factors as well. Salary in this field can afford a comfortable life. Not a life where you are able to say: “I won’t sell 40 hours per week to them anymore” , but “I can have several luxuries other workers are not able to”.

When IT workers start seeing they don’t need to stay inside those expensive cities, they start thinking about leaving it and going to cheaper places. Imagine Dublin salary in Portugal? You can afford a better life and save much more money. Maybe even freeing yourself from the obligation of selling your hours to your boss in a few years. Your boss sees a different opportunity though: they can pay you less and still keep you more or less with the same amount of happiness. Their profit will be greater, your productivity the same, maybe even better, since you are happier for being in a city you chose.

This is not ideal, but it’s not terrible. If you stay in the Global North your lifestyle should be more or less the same. And the truth is: most people in the tech industry are from the Global North.

If you move to the Global South, things change. Let’s use the example of Brazil. Brazilian real is weaker than Euro. €1 = R$ 5,52. [Renting a place costs](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/in/Sao-Paulo): €390,39 , a meal for two in a restaurant:  € 26,77 . Great, so if we grab the average salary and double it, it will be less than the salary in Dublin and you will be able to afford a very good life, right? Well, “a gente não quer só comida, a gente quer bebida, diversão, balé” [1]. How much costs a technical book? Well, The Go Programming Language costs €36,96. How much costs a ticket to the theatre? From €7,25 to €54,35 , What about a concert?  Lollapaloozabr day ticket costs 82 euros. A video-game? The PS4 costs 479 euros at Amazon. A computer? The MacBook 13’’ costs 2264.39 euros.  How much is a the average salary?  448 Euros. So after paying rent,  someone receiving the average wage and living in São Paulo city centre would have 58 Euros. Good news, they can buy a technical book if they don’t eat \o/. Well, at least software engineers make more money right? According to Glassdoor, a software engineer working at NuBank may make 1723,25 Euros per month. Or still according to Glassdoor, an average of 19.203 Euros per year.

Seems like the cost of living in São Paulo is lower than Dublin if you only count renting and eating. If you add technical books, video-games, entertainment the costs are pretty similar. While this are all luxuries, I doubt the developers in the Global North would live without it. So why does those in the Global South should?
